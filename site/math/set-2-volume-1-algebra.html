Concept guide

Multi-Step Linear Equations

Control signs, grouping, fractions, and variables on both sides.

  • Distribute only when it simplifies the structure.
  • Clear fractions with the least common denominator.
  • Check solutions in the original equation.
a(x+b)+c=dax+b=dx+e
Common mistake: A sign error during distribution changes every later step.
Concept guide

Linear and Compound Inequalities

Solve boundaries while preserving direction and endpoint meaning.

  • Reverse the inequality after multiplying or dividing by a negative.
  • Strict inequalities exclude endpoints.
  • Compound inequalities describe intersections of conditions.
L<ax+b<U|x-c|≤r
Common mistake: Do not reverse an inequality when merely adding or subtracting.
Concept guide

Systems of Linear Equations

Use elimination, substitution, and intersections to solve paired relationships.

  • Eliminate the variable with easiest coefficients.
  • A system solution must satisfy both equations.
  • Parallel lines have no solution; identical lines have infinitely many.
a₁x+b₁y=c₁a₂x+b₂y=c₂
Common mistake: Finding one variable is not enough—substitute back for the other.
Concept guide

Linear Functions and Rate of Change

Move fluently among points, slope, intercepts, and contexts.

  • Slope is change in output per unit input.
  • Use a known point to recover the intercept.
  • Average rate of change of a line equals its slope.
y=mx+bm=(y₂−y₁)/(x₂−x₁)
Common mistake: Subtract coordinates in the same order.
Concept guide

Function Notation, Composition, and Inverses

Track inputs and outputs through nested and reversed processes.

  • Composition works from the inside outward.
  • An inverse reverses the original mapping.
  • f⁻¹(y) is an input, not 1/f(y).
(f∘g)(x)=f(g(x))f(f⁻¹(x))=x
Common mistake: Do not reverse the order of composition.
Concept guide

Linear Modeling in Context

Translate fixed values, rates, intersections, and units into equations.

  • Starting value is the intercept.
  • Repeated change is the slope.
  • Break-even occurs where two models are equal.
total=fixed+rate·quantityy₁=y₂ at intersection
Common mistake: Keep units attached to rate and quantity.
Concept guide

Absolute Value Equations and Inequalities

Interpret absolute value as distance from a center.

  • |x-c|=r gives two symmetric points.
  • |x-c|≤r describes an interior interval.
  • |x-c|>r describes two exterior rays.
|x-c|=rc-r≤x≤c+r
Common mistake: A negative right side makes |expression|=negative impossible.
Concept guide

Arithmetic Sequences and Linear Patterns

Treat term number and term value as a linear relationship.

  • The common difference is the sequence slope.
  • The nth term uses n−1 jumps from the first term.
  • Sequence sums use the average of first and last terms.
aₙ=a₁+(n−1)dSₙ=n(a₁+aₙ)/2
Common mistake: Do not use n jumps to reach the nth term.
Concept guide

Piecewise Functions

Select, evaluate, and solve within the correct branch domain.

  • Read the condition before choosing a formula.
  • Boundary equality belongs to only one branch.
  • Solutions must satisfy the branch condition.
f(x)={rule₁, rule₂}
Common mistake: A candidate found algebraically may belong to the wrong branch.
Concept guide

Parameters and Algebraic Reasoning

Use coefficients and structural conditions to determine unknown constants.

  • Infinitely many solutions require identical expressions.
  • No solution requires equal variable coefficients but unequal constants.
  • A point on a graph satisfies its equation.
same slope + same interceptsame coefficient + different constant
Common mistake: Do not solve for x when the question asks for a parameter.

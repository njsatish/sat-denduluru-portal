Concept guide

Quadratic Equations and Roots

Analyze roots using factoring, the quadratic formula, and coefficient relationships.

  • Sum of roots is −b/a.
  • Product of roots is c/a.
  • The discriminant determines the number of real roots.
x=[−b±√(b²−4ac)]/(2a)Δ=b²−4ac
Common mistake: Do not omit the ± in the quadratic formula.
Concept guide

Quadratic Functions and Transformations

Read vertex, axis, opening direction, and transformed values.

  • Vertex form exposes (h,k).
  • The sign of a determines minimum or maximum.
  • The axis of symmetry is x=h.
f(x)=a(x−h)²+kx=−b/(2a)
Common mistake: The horizontal shift sign is opposite the sign inside parentheses.
Concept guide

Polynomials, Factors, and Remainders

Connect factors, roots, remainders, and unknown coefficients.

  • x−r is a factor exactly when P(r)=0.
  • The remainder on division by x−r is P(r).
  • Synthetic substitution is efficient for linear divisors.
P(r)=0remainder=P(r)
Common mistake: Use the zero of the divisor, not the divisor's displayed constant.
Concept guide

Rational Expressions and Equations

Factor before canceling and enforce denominator restrictions.

  • Canceled factors still create excluded values.
  • Multiply equations by the least common denominator.
  • Check solutions against the original denominators.
a/b+c/d=(ad+bc)/bddenominator≠0
Common mistake: Cancel factors, not terms connected by addition.
Concept guide

Radical Expressions and Equations

Simplify square factors and control extraneous roots.

  • Pull perfect-square factors outside radicals.
  • Combine only like radicals.
  • Check every result after squaring.
√(ab)=√a√b√A=B ⇒ A=B² with B≥0
Common mistake: Squaring can introduce solutions that fail the original equation.
Concept guide

Exponential Growth and Decay

Model repeated percent change with multiplicative factors.

  • Growth uses 1+r; decay uses 1−r.
  • Time appears in the exponent.
  • Successive changes multiply.
A(t)=A₀(1+r)^tA(t)=A₀(1−r)^t
Common mistake: Do not multiply the annual percent by time for compound change.
Concept guide

Nonlinear Systems

Find intersections of lines, parabolas, and circles.

  • Set equal expressions that represent the same y.
  • Substitution may produce a quadratic.
  • Tangency corresponds to one repeated intersection.
f(x)=g(x)Δ=0 for tangency
Common mistake: A nonlinear system may have zero, one, or multiple solutions.
Concept guide

Nonlinear Functions, Composition, and Inverses

Compose nonlinear rules and use domain restrictions to define inverses.

  • Evaluate inner functions first.
  • Quadratics need a restricted domain to have an inverse.
  • The inverse swaps inputs and outputs.
(f∘g)(x)=f(g(x))f⁻¹(f(x))=x
Common mistake: Choose the inverse branch consistent with the stated domain.
Concept guide

Equivalent Expressions and Advanced Factoring

Recognize structural identities before expanding.

  • Difference of squares factors into conjugates.
  • Sum and difference of cubes use trinomial factors.
  • Nonmonic quadratics may need the ac method.
a²−b²=(a−b)(a+b)a³−b³=(a−b)(a²+ab+b²)
Common mistake: Sign patterns in cube identities are easy to reverse.
Concept guide

Advanced Parameters and Algebraic Structure

Use roots, discriminants, and perfect-square conditions to solve for parameters.

  • A known root makes the polynomial zero.
  • Repeated roots require discriminant zero.
  • Perfect-square trinomials have a fixed constant relationship.
sum roots=−b/ak=b²/(4a)
Common mistake: Use the structural condition before attempting a full solution.

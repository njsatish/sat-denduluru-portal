Concept guide

Ratios, Rates, Proportions, and Units

Use proportional reasoning and keep units attached throughout the calculation.

  • Find unit rates by dividing.
  • Use conversion factors equal to 1.
  • Scale drawings use corresponding ratios.
rate = quantity/timedensity = mass/volume
Common mistake: Do not mix units before converting them to a common system.
Concept guide

Percentages and Percent Change

Translate percent language into multipliers.

  • Percent change uses the original value as the denominator.
  • Increase: multiply by 1 + r.
  • Decrease: multiply by 1 − r.
new = original(1 ± r)% change = change/original × 100%
Common mistake: A 20% discount followed by 20% markup does not return to the original price.
Concept guide

Statistics and Data Distributions

Summarize center and spread, and reason about changes to data sets.

  • Mean uses every value.
  • Median is the middle ordered value.
  • Standard deviation increases as data spread out.
mean = sum/countrange = maximum − minimum
Common mistake: Sort the data before finding a median.
Concept guide

Scatterplots and Mathematical Models

Interpret associations and linear models without claiming unsupported causation.

  • Slope gives predicted change.
  • Residual = observed − predicted.
  • Correlation describes direction and strength.
ŷ = mx + bresidual = y − ŷ
Common mistake: A strong correlation alone does not prove that one variable causes the other.
Concept guide

Probability and Sampling

Use probability rules, counting, and sound sampling methods.

  • Probability is favorable over total outcomes.
  • Without replacement changes the denominator.
  • Representative samples use random selection across the population.
P(A) = favorable/totalcount = choice₁ × choice₂ × …
Common mistake: Do not treat dependent events as independent.
Concept guide

Area, Perimeter, Surface Area, and Volume

Choose the correct measurement formula and track square or cubic units.

  • Area uses square units.
  • Volume uses cubic units.
  • Area scales by k² and volume by k³.
A△ = ½bhVprism = lwhVcylinder = πr²h
Common mistake: Do not use diameter in a formula that requires radius.
Concept guide

Lines, Angles, and Triangles

Use angle relationships and similarity to find missing measures.

  • Triangle angles sum to 180°.
  • Vertical angles are equal.
  • Corresponding sides of similar triangles are proportional.
exterior angle = remote interior angle₁ + angle₂
Common mistake: Match corresponding sides in the same order.
Concept guide

Right Triangles and Trigonometry

Apply the Pythagorean theorem, special-triangle ratios, and trigonometric ratios.

  • Identify opposite, adjacent, and hypotenuse relative to the given angle.
  • Memorize 45-45-90 and 30-60-90 ratios.
  • Use inverse trig only when finding an angle.
a² + b² = c²sin = opp/hypcos = adj/hyptan = opp/adj
Common mistake: The hypotenuse is always opposite the right angle.
Concept guide

Circles

Work with radius, circumference, area, arcs, sectors, and circle equations.

  • Arc length uses the same fraction of circumference as its central angle.
  • Sector area uses the same fraction of circle area.
  • In a circle equation, the right side is r².
C = 2πrA = πr²(x − h)² + (y − k)² = r²
Common mistake: Do not confuse r with r² in the equation of a circle.
